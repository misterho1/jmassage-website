What Is Deep Tissue Massage?

Pressure That Actually Reaches the Problem

Deep tissue works through the superficial layer to reach the deeper muscle bellies, the myofascia, and the trigger points that refer pain across the body. Slow, sustained pressure applied with thumbs, knuckles, and forearms. The tension in your neck that's been there for two years. The knot in your shoulder that appeared during lockdown and never left. The lower back ache that follows you through every workday at the desk.

Fascia is the connective tissue web that wraps every muscle. Under chronic stress and poor posture, it thickens and adheres, creating the restrictions that gentle massage can't reach. Our Utah-licensed therapists work structurally: identify the source, not the symptom. Most studios in Salt Lake City don't go this deep, or this slow.

Deep tissue or Swedish?

Swedish uses long, flowing strokes at light to medium pressure. It is the right choice for stress relief. Deep tissue uses slower strokes and sustained pressure to reach the deeper muscle layers and the fascia around them. It is the right tool for chronic pain and tension that gentler work has not touched.

Not sure which you need? Tell your therapist where it hurts and how much pressure you like. Our male therapists focus on deep tissue, sports and high-pressure work. Our female therapists are trained across all modalities. You can request either when you book. For the full comparison, read deep tissue vs Swedish massage.

Prefer broad pressure to thumbs and forearms? Ask about Ashiatsu massage. Your therapist holds overhead bars for balance and works with the flat of the foot.

Common Questions

Straight answers about deep tissue.

Will deep tissue massage hurt?

Deep tissue massage involves deliberate, sustained pressure into deeper muscle layers, and it is common to feel intensity, particularly in areas of chronic tension. This is distinct from sharp or alarming pain. Our therapists use the phrase "good hurt" to describe the productive discomfort of releasing long-held tissue. You are always in control: pressure is adjusted throughout the session based on your feedback, and no technique is pushed past your tolerance. Mild soreness 24–48 hours after the session is normal and signals that the tissue is responding. Staying well hydrated before and after helps significantly. If thumb and elbow work feels too sharp, ask about Ashiatsu barefoot massage. The therapist uses the whole foot, which spreads deep pressure over a wider area.

How is deep tissue different from Swedish massage?

Swedish massage uses broad, flowing strokes on superficial muscle layers to promote relaxation and circulation. Deep tissue massage uses slower, more targeted strokes and sustained pressure to reach the deeper layers of muscle and the connective tissue (fascia) surrounding them. Swedish is the right choice for stress relief and general wellbeing; deep tissue is the right tool when you have specific chronic pain, structural tension, scar tissue, or postural dysfunction that hasn't responded to gentler approaches. Many clients benefit from both, used strategically.

How many sessions do I need to see results?

Many clients notice meaningful improvement after a single session, particularly in acute tension areas. For chronic pain patterns that have developed over months or years, a series of four to six sessions is typically recommended, ideally spaced one to two weeks apart. This allows the tissue to integrate between sessions and enables your therapist to work progressively deeper as the musculature releases. Your therapist will assess your specific situation and give you an honest timeline at your first appointment.

What conditions does deep tissue massage help with?

Deep tissue massage is effective for a wide range of conditions: chronic neck and back pain, tension headaches and migraines, repetitive strain injuries, IT band syndrome, rotator cuff issues, postural dysfunction, piriformis syndrome, plantar fasciitis, and recovery from soft tissue injuries. It is also highly effective for the physical toll of sedentary work, forward-head posture, rounded shoulders, and compressed lumbar tissue that accumulate from hours at a desk. When in doubt, consult your therapist, we'll tell you honestly whether deep tissue is appropriate for your situation.

Should I eat before a deep tissue massage?

Avoid a heavy meal within 60–90 minutes of your session. Deep tissue work can involve moderate pressure on the abdomen, and a full stomach makes this uncomfortable. A light snack two hours before is fine. Hydration matters more than food, so drink plenty of water the day before and the day of your session, and keep hydrating well afterward.
